# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Target User

**Primary persona**: Engineering Manager or VP of Engineering at a Series A–B startup with 20–100 employees running 3+ AI tools across the engineering team.

**Why this persona?**

- They control the budget but lack visibility into AI tool usage across individuals
- They're under pressure to optimize burn rate (Series A–B means runway matters)
- They've grown past the "everyone picks their own tools" phase but haven't built procurement processes yet
- 3+ AI tools means there's almost certainly redundancy or overkill — our sweet spot

**Secondary persona**: Tech Lead or Staff Engineer who's frustrated by the tool sprawl and wants to make a case to their manager for consolidation.

---

## Distribution Channels

### Channel 1: SEO + Content (Weeks 1–4)

**Target keywords**: "AI tool spending audit," "Cursor vs Copilot comparison," "reduce AI tool costs," "AI tool stack for startups"

**Content plan**:
- 3 comparison articles: "Cursor vs Copilot vs Windsurf: Which One Should Your Team Use?" (decision-focused, not feature-listicle)
- 1 cost calculator blog post: "How Much Is Your Team Actually Spending on AI Tools?"
- 1 data-driven piece: "We Audited 100 AI Tool Stacks — Here's What We Found" (use aggregated audit data)

**Why**: Engineering managers Google this. The buying intent is high because they're already questioning their spend.

### Channel 2: Dev Communities (Weeks 1–2)

**Targets**: Hacker News (Show HN), r/programming, r/ExperiencedDevs, Dev.to, Twitter/X engineering circles

**Approach**:
- Launch post framing the problem ("We analyzed our AI spend and found we were wasting $1,400/mo")
- Share the tool for free, no sign-up required for the basic audit
- Engage genuinely in comments — no drive-by marketing

**Why**: Our target users live here. A well-written Show HN post can drive 1,000+ audits in a day.

### Channel 3: LinkedIn Outbound (Weeks 3–6)

**Approach**:
- Identify Series A–B startups via Crunchbase/LinkedIn filters (20–100 employees, raised in last 18 months)
- Send personalized connection requests to VP Eng / Head of Eng
- Lead with value: "I ran your team's AI stack through our auditor based on public info — here's what I found"
- Include a screenshot of a sample audit

**Volume**: 20 outbound messages/day, targeting 3% response rate = ~4 conversations/week

### Channel 4: Partnerships (Weeks 4–8)

- **CFO/Finance tools**: Reach out to Ramp, Brex, Airbase — they already surface SaaS spend, we add AI-specific intelligence
- **Engineering blogs/newsletters**: Sponsor or guest-post in The Pragmatic Engineer, ByteByteGo, TLDR Newsletter
- **AI tool vendors**: Counterintuitive, but Cursor/Copilot sales teams might refer customers who are "stuck" evaluating — we help them make a decision faster

## Plan for First 100 Users

| Week | Goal | Action | Target |
|------|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Soft launch | Share in 3 dev Slack communities + Twitter | 20 audits |
| 2 | Show HN | Post to Hacker News, engage all day | 50 audits |
| 3 | Content | Publish first comparison article + LinkedIn outbound begins | 30 audits |
| 4 | Iterate | Analyze audit data, improve rules, publish results post | — |

**Total: 100 audits in 3 weeks.**

### Conversion to Leads

- Audit is free, no sign-up required
- After results, offer: "Get a detailed PDF report + quarterly re-audit → enter email"
- Target: 30% email capture rate = 30 leads from first 100 audits

### Qualifying for Sales

- Teams spending >$500/mo on AI tools = qualified
- Teams with 3+ tools and overspend score >50 = highly qualified
- Follow-up email sequence: Day 1 (report), Day 3 (top recommendation deep-dive), Day 7 (case study / ROI data)

---

## What "Working" Looks Like

- **Week 4**: 100 completed audits, 30 email leads, 5 conversations with qualified prospects
- **Week 8**: 500 audits, clear pattern in which recommendations are most common, first paying customer or LOI
- **Pivot trigger**: If <50 audits in 3 weeks despite active distribution, the problem isn't painful enough — pivot to a different wedge (e.g., usage monitoring instead of spending audit)
